# Pasta, patate e provola
{: .no_toc }

*Source: Guglielmo G.*

*Pasta, patate e provola* is quintessential Neapolitan comfort food. It is creamy, cheesy, and deeply flavorful. Its unique cooking method is more like making risotto than boiling pasta.

## Ingredients

### About 4 servings

- 400 g *pasta mista*; if unavailable, combine broken spaghetti and short pasta shapes
- 2 large Yukon Gold potatoes
- Pancetta or guanciale, as needed
- 1 onion
- 2 celery stalks
- Provola or scamorza
- Parmigiano, as needed
- Basil, as needed
- Olive oil, as needed
- Black pepper
- Salt

## Instructions

1. **Prepare the ingredients:** Mince the pancetta or guanciale almost to a paste. Finely mince the onion and celery. Peel the potatoes and cut them into 1 cm cubes, then hold them in water to prevent browning. Cut the provola or scamorza into tiny cubes and grate a generous handful of Parmigiano.
2. **Boil water:** Bring a pot of lightly salted water to a boil. If you like, add whole pieces of celery, onion, or carrot to make a simple vegetable broth.
3. **Make the soffritto:** In a large pot, begin melting the pancetta over medium heat. Once some fat has rendered, add the onion and celery with salt. Cook over medium to medium-low heat for 5–10 minutes, until softened and lightly colored.
4. **Brown the potatoes:** Add the drained potatoes and season again. Raise the heat to medium or medium-high and stir for 5–10 minutes, letting the flavors mingle and the potatoes brown lightly.
5. **Cook the potatoes:** Add just enough boiling water or broth to cover the potatoes. Cook for exactly 12 minutes.
6. **Cook the pasta:** Add all the pasta and enough hot water or broth to barely cover it. Cook as you would risotto, stirring often to release starch and break up some of the potatoes. Ignore the package time: pasta cooked this way takes longer. A pasta marked 9 minutes may take about 14.
7. **Mantecare:** When the pasta is done, the mixture should be creamy and loose but still dense—slightly looser than macaroni and cheese, not soupy. If it is too wet, raise the heat and cook a little longer; if too firm, add water.
8. Remove from the heat. Add the Parmigiano and provola or scamorza, and stir gently for a few minutes until very creamy. Finish with basil, olive oil, and freshly ground black pepper.

### Cuisine: Western, Italian, Neapolitan

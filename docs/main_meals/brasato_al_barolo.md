# Brasato al Barolo
{: .no_toc }

*Source: Guglielmo G.*

*Brasato al Barolo* is a traditional braised beef dish from Piemonte. The meat and red-wine sauce are the stars, so use the best of each that you can. Barolo is ideal; Barbaresco or another Nebbiolo-based wine such as Gattinara will maintain the character of the dish.

The traditional beef cuts come from the shoulder, neck, or thigh and contain enough fat and connective tissue for a long braise. The ultimate choice is *cappello del prete*—top blade—with its distinct horizontal center vein. *Pesce* and *noce* are also excellent.

## Ingredients

### About 4 servings

- 1 kg beef
- 1 bottle Barolo
- 3 onions
- 3 carrots
- 3 celery stalks
- 1 stick butter
- Simple vegetable broth, as needed
- Olive oil, as needed
- Salt, as needed
- Rosemary, sage, and thyme, as needed
- Bay leaves and whole cloves, as needed

## Instructions

### Marinate

If the butcher has not already done it, trim away the outer fat layer, leaving only a thin film on top. Put the meat in a bowl and cover completely with the wine. Add 1 coarsely chopped onion, 1 carrot, and 1 celery stalk, followed by rosemary, thyme, sage, a couple of bay leaves, and whole cloves. Cover and refrigerate for at least 8 hours and up to 24 hours.

### Sear the beef

1. Remove the meat from the marinade, reserving the marinade, and dry it extremely well.
2. In a heavy Dutch oven or copper pot, heat the butter with a little olive oil; the oil will slow the butter's browning.
3. Sear the meat over medium-high heat until evenly browned, no more than about 5 minutes. Remove the meat and turn off the heat.

### Braise

1. Coarsely chop the remaining onions, carrots, and celery. Turn the heat to low and start a soffritto in the same pot.
2. After about 5 minutes, when the vegetables begin to look glossy, return the meat to the pot and season well with salt.
3. Strain the reserved marinade into the pot, leaving the spent vegetables and herbs behind. Let the alcohol evaporate for a few minutes.
4. Add enough broth to come three quarters of the way up the meat. Turn the heat to its lowest setting, cover, and cook for about 3 hours. Turn the meat every 30–60 minutes so it cooks evenly and does not stick.

### Carve and serve

1. Let the cooked meat cool to room temperature. If time permits, refrigerate the whole pot; colder meat is easier to slice neatly.
2. With a sharp knife, cut the beef into ½–1 cm slices.
3. Pass the sauce through a food mill or blend it until smooth.
4. Place the sliced meat in a pot and cover completely with the sauce. Warm through to melt the fat before serving. Pair with polenta or mashed potatoes.

## Variations

- Use Amarone for a richer, sweeter sauce.
- Replace the top blade with beef cheeks for an especially soft texture.
- If the wine is less robust, add a couple of tablespoons of tomato concentrate or a can of passata.

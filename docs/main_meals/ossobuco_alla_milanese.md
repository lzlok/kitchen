# Ossobuco alla milanese
{: .no_toc }

*Source: Guglielmo G.*

<img class="cookbook-photo" src="{{ '/assets/images/cookbook/ossobuco.JPG' | relative_url }}" alt="Ossobuco alla milanese">

*Ossobuco*—*òss bus* in Milanese dialect—is a quintessential Milanese dish. Although many recipes call for beef shank, veal is the right choice here: it is more tender and a perfect size for an individual portion.

## Ingredients

### About 4 servings

- 4 veal shanks, 1 per person
- 1–2 glasses red wine
- 1 onion
- Olive oil and butter, as needed
- Simple beef stock, as needed
- Flour, as needed
- Thyme, as needed (listed in the original recipe; its use is not specified)
- 1 clove garlic
- 1 lemon
- ½ bunch parsley
- Salt

## Instructions

### Make the beef stock

Make a simple [beef stock]({{ '/docs/soups/beef_stock/' | relative_url }}). Vegetable stock or even water will also work. If using water, bring it to a boil and season it lightly with about 1% salt by weight.

### Build the onion base

Cut the onion into fairly large wedges: halve and clean it, then cut about six wedges from each half. Heat a flat, roughly 12-inch sauté pan over medium heat for a few minutes. Add a little olive oil and all the onion. Cook the wedges quickly and intensely so the tips begin to char. The onion is there to add depth and will not be part of the final dish.

### Prepare and sear the veal

1. Make 2–3 small cuts around the edge of each shank to stop it curling as it cooks, then flour both sides lightly.
2. When the onion is ready, add a generous amount of butter or oil and put in the veal.
3. Sear over high heat for a couple of minutes on each side.

### Add the wine and stock

1. After searing the second side, deglaze with the wine. Wait 1–2 minutes for the alcohol to evaporate.
2. Add enough stock to almost cover the veal. Cover, reduce the heat to low, and cook for about 1 hour, turning the shanks once halfway through.
3. The veal should be tender and the sauce velvety, spoonable, and slightly thick.

### Make the gremolata and serve

1. Remove the veal from the pan and take out most of the onion; strain the sauce if you want a more refined finish.
2. Chop the parsley and garlic together very finely, then mix in a little lemon zest.
3. Stir the gremolata gently into the sauce off the heat.
4. Return the veal to the pan and coat it with the sauce. Serve hot with extra sauce spooned over the top.

### Cuisine: Western, Italian, Milanese
